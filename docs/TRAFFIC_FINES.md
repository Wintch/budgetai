# Traffic fines in Argentina: where to check, how they are calculated, how to pay the least (general)

Method notes, written 2026-10-19/20, focused on **spending as little as possible**. General only, nothing about a person.
Sources are news, agency pages and legislative drafts found by search; **the laws themselves were not read** and nothing was tested against a real account.
Everything marked "verify" must be confirmed on the official site or the coupon before acting. Rules change: dates and amounts are examples.

## 1. Why it pays to look early

- A fine is a number of **unidades fijas (UF)**; the pesos value is the UF of the day, and the UF is **updated every two months**, following the price of premium fuel.
  Examples from news, province of Buenos Aires: 1,336 (Nov-Dec 2024), 1,398 (Jan-Feb 2025), 1,711 to 1,896 (Mar-Apr 2026), 2,281 (Sept-Oct 2026).
  An unpaid fine is **revalued at each update**: a 100 UF fine is 189,600 at 1,896 and 228,100 at 2,281 (calc), a 20% rise in six months.
- A reduction for **voluntary payment** exists only at the start (see section 5); waiting loses it.
- An old fine can block the **licence renewal** and, in practice, a **vehicle transfer**; the debt stays with the person who committed the infraction (secondary source).
- Late interest can be added on an expired coupon (the coupon cites an article of the fiscal code; whether and how much applies to traffic fines is **not confirmed**).

## 2. Where to check (all official lookups are free; do not pay a "gestor" or an app to look)

Each jurisdiction keeps its own record, so check **every place the vehicle has been driven**:

| where | what it covers | how | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Province of Buenos Aires: `infraccionesba.gba.gob.ar`, "Consulta de infracciones" | provincial roads and its municipalities | by plate ("dominio") or by document number | has a **reCAPTCHA**: a person must do it. Shows the court, the coupon and the cause state |
| City of Buenos Aires: the City's "consulta de infracciones" (page under `buenosaires.gob.ar`; old links break, find the current one from the City's site) | the City | by plate or document number | the status "A resolver en UACF" only means it is with the controller; the discount can still apply (secondary source) |
| National: `consultainfracciones.seguridadvial.gob.ar` (road-safety agency; the national system SINAI); the national traffic record certificate (CENAT) is a related record | national routes and the jurisdictions adhered to the national agreement | by plate or document number | address confirmed live by a user on 2026-10-20. In that user's case it showed **no fines while a provincial fine existed**, so a clean national result **does not replace the local lookups** (one case, not a rule) |
| Other provinces and municipalities | their own | each has a site | check the one where you drove |
| Unofficial apps (one gathers 30+ jurisdictions) | repackages the official data | | free, but **verify on the official site**; never enter more than a plate |

Rule: use the plate (not the ID number) when you can; keep the plate out of public repos and chats you do not control.

## 3. Reading a result (province of Buenos Aires example)

| field | meaning |
|---|---|
| record (acta) number | the id of the fine; quote it in every message to the court |
| generated / coupon due date | when the coupon was made and when its voluntary or normal payment window ends |
| coupon state | paid, pending, **expired** |
| cause state | the state of the case, separate from the coupon: for example "dispute pending validation" = a defence (descargo) was filed and has not been decided; ask the court office for the exact meaning |
| amount | UF x UF value **of the day the page is shown**, so it can differ from the printed coupon |
| infraction code and description | what the fine is for |
| court (radicacion) | which office holds the case; this is who to call |
| interest | recalculated **when the coupon is printed** again |

## 4. How the amount is calculated

amount = UF count set for the infraction x current UF value. Serious ones such as running a red light start at 300 UF and can reach 1,000 UF (secondary source).
Each +100 pesos in the UF adds 100 pesos per UF of the fine: 30,000 on a 300 UF fine (calc).

## 5. How to pay the least (the decision)

1. **Check the plate at the start and every two months** (the UF update rhythm) or before any procedure (licence, transfer, sale). It costs nothing and an early fine can be paid at half price.
2. **Guilty, or not worth arguing: pay inside the voluntary period.** About 50% off (reported: province about 30 business days from the notice, the City about 40 days; **verify on the coupon**). An expired coupon loses it.
3. **Filing a defence (descargo) loses that discount.** So the choice is a bet, and it can be calculated: paying now costs 0.5 x F; disputing costs (1 - p) x F', where F is today's amount, F' the amount when it is decided
   (higher if the UF rises) and p the chance of winning. Disputing only pays when p > 1 - 0.5 x F / F', which is **more than 50%** and more the longer it takes (calc). Dispute only with strong evidence.
4. **If you dispute:** no form is required in the province, but include evidence, the record number, the owner's name and ID, and the plate; there is an online platform for defences (since 2019), and postal mail with
   acknowledgement for those living more than 60 km away (secondary source; verify). In the City the defence goes by the official phone line or in person with an appointment at the infractions office (verify).
   Keep the receipt, copies of the evidence (dated photos, street views, anything showing signage, lights, speed limits) and the answers in writing.
5. **If you lose and cannot pay at once: a payment plan.** Reported: 6 or 12 instalments with no surcharge, at the **UF of the day of joining**, so joining early freezes the price while the UF rises (verify it is still in force).
6. **Do not rely on prescription.** Reported: 2 years for minor faults and 5 for serious ones (national traffic law, art. 89; the province follows the same criterion), but the clock is **interrupted** by proceedings and a new serious infraction restarts it.
   An open case can move at any time.
7. **A fixed-camera ticket** has a notice deadline: a provincial draft quoting the law says the postal notice must arrive within 60 business days of the infraction or the action lapses (**not confirmed in force**). Compare the infraction date with the date the notice arrived.
8. **Buying or selling a used vehicle:** check the plate in every jurisdiction, ask the seller to pay or settle first, and keep proof; a fine follows the person who committed it, but it can complicate the transfer (verify).
9. **Never pay a fine you are disputing without asking the court first**, and never pay through a link in a message: pay from the official site or the printed coupon.

## 6. Laws to read before relying on any of this

Ley 24.449 (national traffic law) and Ley 26.363; Ley 13.927 (province of Buenos Aires, adhesion) and its regulation, Decreto 532/09 (fines in UF); the fiscal code of the province (Ley 10.397) for the interest article the coupon cites;
Resolucion 69/20 (payment plan); the transport ministry's bimonthly resolution that sets the UF. Official texts: the province's legislation site (SIN) and the Boletin Oficial.

## 7. Not verified

The exact current voluntary-payment windows; whether the payment plan is still open; whether interest runs on a traffic fine while a defence is pending; the minor/serious classification of each infraction; which jurisdictions feed the national lookup (a provincial fine did not appear in one case).
