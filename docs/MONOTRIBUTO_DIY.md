# Monotributo without paying for routine accounting: what must be done, when (general)

Written 2026-10-20 from press and blogs found by search; **the official tax-agency pages and the regulations were not read**, and amounts, caps and dates change every semester. Not tax advice: use it to know what to ask and what to check on the official site.
Applies to the simplified tax regime for small independent workers in Argentina (monotributo), with the province of Buenos Aires unified scheme as the example.

## 1. What the regime asks of you (as reported)

| duty | how often | how | consequence if skipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pay the unified monthly quota** (national tax, pension contribution, health-insurance contribution; in the province of Buenos Aires the provincial gross-income tax is reported as folded into the same payment) | **monthly, due around the 20th** | electronic payment voucher (VEP) or **direct debit** from the tax-agency site | interest on the debt; **after 10 consecutive unpaid periods the agency removes you from the regime** without notice, and you cannot return for two years (you pay under the general regime: VAT and income tax) |
| **Issue an electronic invoice of type C** for every sale or service | each sale | the agency's "Comprobantes en linea" service or its free invoicing app; first create a **point of sale** (4 or 5 digits) in the agency's registry | fines; you cannot prove income |
| **Recategorise** (check that your last 12 months of income fit your category) | **twice a year**, in January (deadline reported early February) and July or August; those registered less than six months before are exempt | the "Recategorizarme" service with tax id and fiscal key; if you have electronic invoicing and 12 full months the platform suggests a category | reported **fine of 50%** of the integrated tax and pension contribution for the real category, retroactive adjustments, loss of benefits. Sources disagree on whether it is mandatory for everyone or only for those who exceed their category: **verify** |
| **Keep within your category's income cap** (and the other parameters your category has) | always | track the sum of invoices of the last 12 months | exclusion from the regime if you exceed the highest cap |
| Periodic sworn declarations | the unified scheme is reported to **remove** the periodic provincial declaration; the national regime has none for the monotributista. **Exception: if you are registered in provincial gross-income tax under the Convenio Multilateral (typical when clients are in other provinces), a monthly return (form CM03, SIFERE Web) and an annual one (CM05) are due, and the provincial tax is not folded into the quota** | | read your registration certificate: it lists the provincial taxes and jurisdictions; check with the agency if you have other registered taxes or a municipal fee |

That is **one payment a month, an invoice per sale, and two checks a year**, all doable online with a fiscal key of level 3. An accountant is **not obligatory** in the regime; one source says it is recommended when you might have to move to the general regime.

## 2. Amounts (examples only; verify the current table on the agency's site)

- Totals for the services column, reported for May-June 2026: category A about 42,387, B about 48,251, C about 56,502, D about 72,414 per month. The caps were raised by about 14% in February 2026 and amounts change again each semester; the sources disagree on the exact dates and figures.
- In the province of Buenos Aires the unified scheme adds the provincial part to the same payment (reported: from August 2026 the new provincial amounts are included in the voucher or the direct debit).
- Check which amount you really owe in the "Cuenta Corriente de Monotributistas y Autonomos" service.

## 3. The calendar to keep (copy into any calendar app)

- **Every month, by the 18th:** check that the payment is set (direct debit active, or generate and pay the voucher); keep the receipt.
- **Every sale:** issue the type C invoice the same day.
- **Early January and early July (check the exact dates each year):** add up the invoices of the last 12 months, compare with your category, and recategorise if needed. Do it before the deadline (reported early February and in August).
- **Every semester:** re-read the new table; the amounts change.
- **Every year, on your registration anniversary:** check you still qualify for your category and activity.

## 3b. Three things that are easy to get wrong

1. **Do not hand over your fiscal key: delegate.** The key is personal and not transferable. The official way for an accountant to act for you is the **"Administrador de Relaciones"** service: you enter with your own key, choose the services, enter the accountant's tax id and confirm; the accountant then **accepts the designation** from their own account. You can cancel it the same way. Someone who has your key can see and change everything, including the contact data tied to the account. If a key was shared, delegate first, then **change the key** and check the contact email and phone.
2. **Work paid in dollars: which invoice and which exchange rate.** Invoices to a **local** client are type C, in pesos. Services sold to a client **abroad** are reported as an export of services, invoiced with **type E** (on a separate export point of sale), in foreign currency or pesos, converted at the central-bank-of-the-nation buyer rate of the previous day (one source says the seller rate: verify). Sources disagree on whether export income counts against the category cap, and a currency-exchange rule on how much foreign income may be kept without selling it (a figure of 36,000 dollars a year was reported for monotributistas) must be checked in the central bank's current rule. **If your clients are abroad, ask an accountant once.** Clients in **other provinces of the same country** are still local clients (type C); the open question is the **provincial gross-income tax**: whether the province of the client can claim anything or apply withholdings when you work from another province (not verified; ask an accountant once, and check your invoices for withholding certificates).
3. **Your category depends on your real invoiced pesos of the last 12 months, not on your dollars.** Add up the pesos on every invoice issued (the agency's "Mis Comprobantes" service lists them and exports a spreadsheet) and compare with the current cap table. If your category is lower than that total, recategorise upward before the deadline; above the highest cap you are excluded. When income drops, you can recategorise **downward** at the next window, but the saving between neighbouring categories is small (a few thousand pesos a month in the figures above).

## 3c. Months without income

The quota is due even with no work, and removal after 10 unpaid periods is the main risk. **Leaving the regime voluntarily to save the quota is not clear-cut**: no source found explained the voluntary procedure, whether you must be up to date, or what happens to the health-insurance and pension contributions; one source says a person removed for debt may need to wait before re-registering (three calendar years, conflicting with other sources that say it is allowed after paying). You need the regime active to invoice any new job. Ask the agency or an accountant before leaving, and do not leave while any debt is open.

## 4. If you are leaving a paid accountant

1. **Before stopping, ask for a handover**: your current category and the income used in the last recategorisation; the list of taxes and activities registered in your name (the agency's registration certificate); the dates of the next windows; how to issue invoices and where the point of sale is; a one-hour walkthrough.
2. **Own your access**: you must hold your own fiscal key (level 3), and know which services you delegated to the accountant (the "administrator of relations" service shows them); you can revoke them.
3. **Pay for what you need, not for a monthly retainer**: ask whether they will do just the two yearly checks, or answer questions by the hour.
4. Do not leave an unpaid fee dispute that could make an accountant keep your access; agree the handover first.
5. Set **reminders** for the monthly payment and the two windows, and automate the payment (direct debit) so that forgetting costs nothing.

## 5. If you fall behind

Interest accrues daily on the debt; the system blocks procedures such as recategorisation while there are unpaid periods; a payment plan exists for monotributo debt (reported: available from the month the second unpaid quota falls due, once, via the current-account service or the plans service: **terms not verified**). Pay the oldest periods first and keep the monthly payment current.

## 6. Not verified
