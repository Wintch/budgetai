# Supermarket loyalty programs: what they are, what they cost in data, whether they pay (general)

Written 2026-10-20 from the chains' public legal pages found by search and from press. General only, nothing about a person.
**The legal texts change and several pages are old**: read the current terms of a program before joining; what follows is a reading aid, not legal advice. Nothing was tested by registering.

## 1. What they are

Free membership programs (a card, an app, a coupon list) offered by the big chains: **Comunidad Coto**, **Mi Carrefour**, **ClubDia**, the Cencosud ones (**Jumbo Mas**, **Vea Ahorro**, and a premium tier), and similar ones elsewhere. The program gives personal coupons or weekly discounts; in exchange the chain links purchases to an identity.
Do not confuse them with the **payment-method promotions** (bank card, wallet, weekday discounts), which are separate and often larger.

## 2. Why a company gives a discount for a click

The discount is the price of the data. In the terms that were read:
- **Coto (the program's rules):** the data of the applicant and the member and the card transactions go into a database that the company may use for advertising, promotion and commercial purposes, and may **transfer to agents or third parties** for those purposes; the member consents on joining. Rights: update, correct, delete, and free access every six months; marketing can be revoked in writing, on the program's page or by phone.
- **ClubDia:** the registration asks for a user name, full name, an **ID number** and contact data; stated purposes include marketing; the data is not sold or rented except as the policy says, and the policy says the file **resides in the United States** (transfers to other countries are possible). Coupons are personal, non-transferable, free.
- **Cencosud (Jumbo, Disco, Vea):** data given on any one site may be used by **all the group's business units**; consent can be revoked for some or all units; loyalty numbers are collected; one banner describes a **biometric validation** step (it says it does not store facial images).
- **Carrefour (online terms and the panel):** the user authorises processing for site administration, identity checks and **promotional profiling**, with possible transfer to third parties; removal is requested by email.
- Press (a business-media analysis of loyalty algorithms): the main benefit for the retailer is information, to know customers and win share. A purchase history tied to an ID reveals habits, health and income level.
What the retailer gets: personalised pricing and coupons, better stock and promotion planning, and the ability to sell insights or ad space to its suppliers (a retail-media business; general knowledge, not in the pages above).

## 3. What they pay (reported, not verified now)

- Discounts depend on **day, payment method and card**, not just on being a member: for example (press, 2025-2026) a store credit card with a weekly 20% day, 10% for pensioners or social-security beneficiaries with a **monthly cap**, 15% on a digital account day. Rates and caps change.
- Cumulative rules vary: some discounts do not stack with bank promotions (one program's older rules said so). Press says the same shopping list can differ by about 20% or more between chains, days and payment methods.
- Shelf-price gaps between chains can be bigger than a loyalty discount (see `GROCERY_PLANNER.md` s.1a: a median gap of about 24% between the cheapest and dearest chain for staples in a first probe).

## 4. Is it worth it? A way to decide (calc, per household)

1. Compute the weekly basket **at shelf price** in each chain (the planner does this from the official data).
2. Add each program's discount **only where you would really meet its conditions** (the day, the card, the cap) and mark it `requires_membership` / `payment_method` in the data (`STANDARDS.md` s.4).
3. Net saving per month = the cheapest basket with membership discounts minus the cheapest basket without them. If it is **small** (for example under a few thousand pesos a month), the data is not worth giving. If it is **large**, join only that chain.
4. Prefer the discounts that need **no identity**: shop the day a payment method gives a discount, buy own-brand items, and compare unit prices.
5. If you join: use the **minimum data** (an email used only for this, no optional fields, no biometric step), refuse the marketing and third-party transfer boxes where the form allows it, never give the card or the phone number for anything that is not needed, and keep a note of where you registered so you can exercise your rights.

## 5. Your rights (Ley 25.326, the data-protection law, as the chains' own texts invoke it)

Access to your data (free at regular intervals), correction, deletion and blocking, and revoking consent for marketing. The control body is the national access-to-information agency (some chain texts still name an older office). To ask, write to the data contact in each program's policy; keep the reply.

## 6. If registration does not work

Common causes (guesses, not verified): the ID or phone is already linked to another account, an SMS or email validation fails, a biometric or document check does not accept the ID, the form requires a field you cannot give. Try the store counter or the chain's support line, note the exact error, and compare with the benefit you expect before spending effort.
